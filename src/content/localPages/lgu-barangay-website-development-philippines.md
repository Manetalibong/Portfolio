Residents do not visit an **LGU website** to be impressed by animation. They want office hours, a downloadable form, a hotline, or an announcement they can open on a phone while lining up. A **barangay website developer in the Philippines** should design for that reality first.

I am Mane Talibong. I build **government website design in the Philippines** projects for local units that need something usable, not a forgotten domain from a previous administration.

## What residents and offices usually need

Common requirements I plan for:

- A home page that points to the most-used services, not a wall of seals
- Announcements and advisories with dates, so people can tell what is current
- An officials directory and office list with roles, not only posed photos
- Contact details that work: phone, email, and a map or landmark
- Space for transparency-related content your office already publishes (bids, reports, or a transparency board), structured so files are findable

I cannot issue a legal certificate that a site “passes” every circular. I can help you structure pages so the information citizens and auditors look for is easier to find and update.

## Accessibility and mobile-first for the public

Most constituents will never open the site on a desktop in an air-conditioned office. They will use prepaid data. **LGU website developer** work has to keep text readable, tap targets large, and PDFs labeled clearly.

That includes:

- Pages that work in both English and Filipino where your office already communicates that way
- Contrast and heading structure that help people scan
- A layout staff can update without breaking the whole homepage

If your team is not technical, WordPress is often the right CMS. See [WordPress development](../wordpress-developer-butuan-city/).

## Working with government process

If your office needs a proposal, a scope of work, or documentation that fits procurement, say so early. I am willing to work within those processes: written quotes, itemized deliverables, and revision of page lists after a TWG or IT officer reviews.

Timelines should be honest. Content gathering (photos of officials, ordinance lists, hotlines) often takes longer than the code. A good **barangay website** still needs someone inside the LGU assigned to send updates.
