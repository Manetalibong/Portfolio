## Features that move inquiries

A useful listing site usually includes:

- Property pages with photo galleries, price, location, and key specs
- Search and filters (city, type, bedrooms, budget range) so users are not scrolling a dump of posts
- Inquiry forms on each listing, with the property name passed through so you know which house they meant
- Map or landmark context, especially when addresses are “near the gym” more than a clean street number
- Agent or office contact that stays visible on mobile

The **property listing website** should also load quickly. Heavy slideshows that freeze a mid-range Android phone will cost you the next tap.

## The site as a lead engine

Facebook remains part of real estate marketing. The website’s job is to catch people who searched Google, clicked a tracked link, or wanted to browse without joining a group.

That means:

- Unique listing URLs you can send in chat
- Clear next steps: schedule a viewing, request a brochure, or ask for financing notes
- Capture of name, number, and which property, so follow-up is not guesswork

## Mobile is not optional

Most property searches start on a phone. Galleries must swipe. Filters must be usable with a thumb. Click-to-call should sit near the price. A desktop-only **real estate website** will look finished in your office and fail on site visits.

I build mobile-first and then check the desktop layout, not the reverse.
