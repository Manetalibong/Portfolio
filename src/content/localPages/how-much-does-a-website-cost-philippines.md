## Website design price in the Philippines

When people type **website design price Philippines** or **website design philippines price**, they usually want a brochure or small-business site: home, services, about, contact, mobile layout, and a way to inquire.

## Three ways people pay for a site

**DIY website builders.** Tools like Wix, Squarespace, or a drag-and-drop WordPress theme keep the monthly fee visible. You pay with time: learning the editor, fixing mobile layout, writing your own copy, and figuring out SEO later. DIY can work for a hobby, a very simple calling-card page, or a test. It gets expensive when you fight the builder for a year, then pay someone to rebuild. See [website builder vs custom websites](../blog/website-builder-vs-custom-websites/).

**Freelance web developers.** You pay for skill and attention, usually without office overhead. Quality varies. Ask for live URLs, not only mockups. A good **freelance web developer in Butuan** should explain scope in pages, not in mysterious “packages” that hide hosting and revisions. See [freelance web developer](../freelance-web-developer-butuan/).

**Agencies.** You pay for process, extra hands, and sometimes a brand team. That can be right for large companies. For a five-page local business site, you may be funding layers you will never meet. Compare that to [affordable website design for small businesses](../affordable-website-design-butuan/) if budget is the main constraint.

None of these is “the correct” **website price in the Philippines**. The correct one matches the job.

## What actually drives cost

When someone asks **magkano gumawa ng website**, I walk through drivers, not a single magic number.

**Number of pages.** Home plus contact is a different job from ten service pages, a blog, and unique town pages. More unique layouts means more design and content decisions.

**Ecommerce.** A catalog, payments, shipping rules, and inventory is closer to an operations tool than a brochure. GCash, Maya, bank transfer, and COD-friendly flows take planning. See [ecommerce website development](../ecommerce-website-development-butuan/) and the deeper guide on [ecommerce website price in the Philippines](../blog/ecommerce-website-price-philippines/).

**Custom design vs a starting theme.** A theme can be a smart way to keep **budget web design in the Philippines** under control *if* it is customized so it does not look like five other shops. Full custom design costs more because every section is decided on purpose.

**WordPress vs simpler sites.** WordPress is often worth it when you will edit content yourself. It also needs updates. Factor [WordPress development](../wordpress-developer-butuan-city/) plus later [maintenance](../services/website-maintenance/).

**SEO as part of the build.** Titles, local pages, and Google Business Profile work add time. They also protect you from launching a pretty site that never shows up. See [website design and SEO services](../website-design-seo-services-butuan/) and [local SEO services in the Philippines](../local-seo-services-philippines/).

**Content and photos.** If you have none, someone has to write, shoot, or source them. That is real work, whether billed by me or done by your team at night after the shop closes.

**Ongoing costs.** Domain, hosting, premium plugins, email, and small edits after launch. A cheap launch with no plan for updates is how sites get hacked or stuck on PHP versions nobody should still run.

## Rough tiers by complexity (not a price list)

Think in bands of complexity rather than memorizing a peso figure from a 2022 Facebook comment.

- **Very simple:** one to three pages, existing logo, you supply copy and photos.
- **Standard local business:** several service pages, mobile-first layout, contact paths, basic on-page SEO.
- **Growth:** WordPress, blog or resources, location pages, stronger local SEO.
- **Store or special workflow:** ecommerce, bookings, membership, or LGU-style directories.

Niche needs also change the band: [LGU and barangay sites](../lgu-barangay-website-development-philippines/), [school and church sites](../school-church-website-design-philippines/), and [real estate listing sites](../real-estate-website-development-philippines/) are not the same as a three-page brochure.

## How to compare quotes without getting burned

Ask each provider:

1. What is in the page list?
2. Who writes the copy?
3. Is hosting included for year one?
4. How many revision rounds?
5. What happens after launch?
6. Is SEO in the build or a separate retainer?

If two quotes differ wildly, the scopes are probably different. A low number that excludes content, mobile QA, and SEO is not cheaper. It is unfinished.
