## What “maintenance” should include

A useful **WordPress maintenance Philippines** plan usually covers:

- Core, theme, and plugin updates (on a schedule, not panic mode)
- Backups you can actually restore
- Security checks (malware scans, login hardening basics)
- Uptime or “is the site up?” monitoring at a simple level
- Small content edits (swap a photo, change hours, add a promo line)
- A direct line when something breaks

It should not be a black box labeled “SEO package” that never touches the install.

## What changes the cost

**Platform.** WordPress with many plugins costs more attention than a simple static site.

**Ecommerce.** Stores with payment plugins and inventory need more careful updates. A broken checkout costs sales the same day.

**Edit volume.** Five text changes a month is not the same as weekly landing page launches.

**Hosting quality.** Cheap shared hosting with no staging makes every update riskier. Sometimes the right “maintenance” spend is better hosting first.

**Age of the site.** Old PHP, abandoned themes, and stacked page builders take cleanup time before a calm retainer makes sense.

## Monthly retainer vs one-off fixes

**Retainer.** Best when the site is a sales tool you cannot afford to leave dark. You pay for prevention and a known response path.

**One-off rescue.** Fine for a single emergency. Expensive if you call every month because nothing was maintained.

**Project + handoff.** Some owners want training and a checklist, then self-manage. That works if someone on your team will actually click “update” and keep backups.

## DIY maintenance vs hiring help

You can maintain a simple WordPress site yourself if you:

- Keep a backup before every update
- Update on a calm week, not during a big promo
- Remove plugins you do not use
- Watch for PHP and SSL warnings from the host

Hire help when the site makes money, when ecommerce is involved, or when nobody on the team wants that job. **Website maintenance cost in the Philippines** is often cheaper than a weekend emergency rebuild.

## Questions to ask any maintenance provider

1. What is updated, and how often?
2. Where are backups stored, and how do restores work?
3. What counts as a “small edit” vs a billable project?
4. Do you stage updates before pushing live?
5. What is the response time if checkout or contact forms fail?
