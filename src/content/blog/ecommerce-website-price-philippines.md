## Why ecommerce costs more than a brochure site

A five-page business site is mostly pages and contact paths. An **online store** is closer to an operations tool:

- Product catalog with photos, variants, and stock rules
- Checkout that Philippine buyers trust
- Payment options that match how people actually pay
- Shipping or pickup rules
- Order emails and a way for you to manage stock after launch

That is why **ecommerce website price in the Philippines** sits in a higher band than a simple calling-card site.

## What usually drives the quote

**Catalog size.** Ten products with simple sizes is not the same as two hundred SKUs with colors, bundles, and wholesale pricing.

**Payments.** Filipino buyers expect GCash, Maya, bank transfer, and often COD-friendly flow. Card gateways add setup and fees. Each path needs clear copy on mobile.

**Shipping.** Flat rates, Lalamove-style pickup notes, provincial shipping tables, and free-shipping thresholds all take planning.

**Platform.** WooCommerce on WordPress is common for SMEs who want control. Other stacks can fit, but plugins, hosting, and updates become part of the ongoing cost.

**Design.** A theme you customize is cheaper than a full custom shop UI. Custom is worth it when branding and conversion matter more than speed to launch.

**Integrations.** Inventory sync, accounting exports, or marketplace feeds raise scope fast.

**Content.** If you have no product photos or descriptions, someone has to create them. That work is real whether you do it or I do.

## Rough complexity tiers (not a price list)

Use these to sanity-check quotes:

- **Starter shop:** small catalog, simple payments, pickup or basic shipping, you supply photos.
- **Growth shop:** more products, collections, clearer checkout, basic SEO, training so you can add items.
- **Operations shop:** larger catalog, stricter inventory, more payment options, shipping rules, and a maintenance plan.

If a quote looks “too cheap,” ask what is excluded: product upload, payment setup, mobile QA, SEO, or training.

## Ecommerce vs Facebook selling

Facebook is still how many Philippine brands start. A store does not replace Messenger. It reduces repeated questions and gives you a link you can put in ads, Google, and packaging.

Local SEO still matters if you also have a physical pickup point. Pair the store with [local SEO services in the Philippines](../local-seo-services-philippines/) when Maps and “near me” drive foot traffic.

## What to ask before you hire

1. How many products are included in the first upload?
2. Which payment methods are in scope?
3. Who writes product copy?
4. Is hosting and SSL included for year one?
5. What happens after launch (updates, backups, plugin breaks)?
6. Is SEO part of the build?
