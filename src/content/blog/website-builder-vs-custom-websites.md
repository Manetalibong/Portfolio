People type **website builder** into Google when they want something live this weekend. They type **website maker** when they want a person who can actually shape the site. They type **websites** when they are comparing both.

I am Mane Talibong, a web developer in Butuan City. I also served as Editor in Chief of The Watch Guild (TWG), the official student publication of PECIT. The homepage photo with this post is the kind of site a newsroom actually uses: nav for About, handbook, journalists, research, and articles, a hero for the school identity, and a Spotlight Stories row that has to change every week.

A drag-and-drop **website builder** can get a logo and three photos online. It rarely survives a real publication calendar.

## What a website builder is good for

A **website builder** (Wix, Squarespace, a free subdomain tool, or a theme you never customize) is useful when the job is small:

- You need a link for a calling card or Facebook bio
- You have one offer and you will not publish weekly
- You are testing an idea before you spend on a proper build

For that, a builder is honest. You pay with time spent fighting the editor, or with a monthly fee. The **websites** that come out of it look like other starter sites, and that is fine if trust is already handled in person.

Trouble starts when the site has to do journalism work, school work, or sales work on a phone, every week.

## What The Watch Guild page is actually doing

Look at that layout as a reader, not as a template shopper.

**Header:** the publication name, a line that it is PECIT's official student paper, and social icons. Visitors should know in one second whose **website** this is.

**Nav:** Home, About Us, Student Handbook, Contact Us, Journalist, Research, Articles. That is a content system. Those are sections that editors will fill all year. A builder "seven buttons" page that does not have a plan for article archives will rot by mid-semester.

**Hero:** the PECIT crest, the school name, a slogan, and a tagline over a campus photo. Institutional **websites** have to carry a crest without looking like a stolen logo on a generic theme.

**Spotlight Stories:** three featured pieces with different artwork. A paper that writes "Truth should be written," campus essays, and photo-led stories needs a homepage that can swap features without a developer sitting next to the editor every Friday. That is either a CMS you can train staff on, or it is unpaid overtime.

I know that workflow because I sat in the newsroom. Editors do not want to log into a **website builder** that hides the article list three clicks deep and breaks mobile the first time someone pastes from Docs.

## Website maker vs website builder

A **website maker**, in the way people actually hire one, is a person who decides structure, design, and how the team will update it.

| If you hire a builder product | If you hire a website maker |
| --- | --- |
| You pick a template and hope it fits | Pages match how readers search and how staff work |
| Updates depend on the tool's limits | Updates depend on a simple editor you were trained on |
| SEO is an afterthought plugin | Titles, headings, and internal links are planned |
| It looks "online" | It looks like your school, shop, or paper |

Custom still does not have to mean expensive animation. For TWG-style **websites**, the craft is information architecture: where articles live, how the handbook is reached, and how Spotlight Stories rotate.

If you want to edit after launch, [WordPress development](../../services/wordpress-development/) is the usual stack. If the site is a campus, parish, or organization with non-technical staff, see [school and church website design](../../school-church-website-design-philippines/).

## When websites outgrow a builder

Switch off the **website builder** when you notice any of these:

- You publish more than a few pages a month
- Several people need to log in (editors, officers, teachers)
- You need a handbook, directory, research list, or article archive
- Mobile readers bounce because the hero image and nav fight each other
- Google cannot tell your About page from your homepage

Local businesses hit the same wall. A sari-sari page can start on a builder. A company that needs services, [local SEO](../../services/local-seo/), and a site that loads on prepaid data usually needs a [website design and development](../../services/website-design-development/) pass.

## How I approach these websites

I start with who updates the site, not which template is on sale.

1. List the real sections (home, about, contact, plus whatever you publish).
2. Decide the homepage modules: one hero, a featured row, a path to archives.
3. Build mobile-first. Publication readers and parents are on phones.
4. Train one person to add an article or Spotlight card without breaking layout.
5. Leave room for SEO so the **websites** can be found for the school name, the paper name, and the stories.

That is the **website maker** job. The tool is secondary.
